# A Business Address That Isn't Your Home

**Prepared:** November 6, 2026
**Goal:** Use a non-home address in outreach emails (CAN-SPAM) and on business paperwork, and move the home address off the public business record.

## Recommendation

Get **one virtual mailbox with a real street address** (about $10 to $15 a month), and pair it with a **registered agent service** (about $125 a year) when you update your state filing.

- **This week:** sign up for iPostal1 or Anytime Mailbox, pick a location near you, and complete USPS Form 1583 (notarized online during signup). Put that address in every outreach email.
- **This month:** file your state's change-of-address form, putting the new mailbox address as the principal or mailing address and a registered agent service in the registered agent field.

## Two different problems

| Problem | Fix | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Outreach emails need a physical address (CAN-SPAM) | Any USPS PO box or a private mailbox (CMRA) registered under USPS rules | The FTC's 2008 rule says an accurately registered PO box or private mailbox satisfies the "valid physical postal address" requirement. |
| Home address is on the public business record | Registered agent service + a street-address mailbox, then a state amendment | The state record has separate fields (registered agent, principal office, mailing). Each one that shows your home address must be replaced. |

## Why not just a PO box

For cold email alone, a PO box is legal and cheapest. But you resell POS and processing, so you'll also put this address on merchant applications, ISO agreements, bank accounts, and possibly your state filing. Many of those want a street address. One mailbox with a street address covers everything, for a few dollars more.

## Format it correctly

Write private mailbox addresses the way USPS expects:

```
Your Business Name
123 Main St, PMB 456
City, ST 12345
```

Use "PMB" or "#" for the box number. Don't present it as a "Suite", which misrepresents the address.

## Caveats

- **Past filings stay public.** Updating your address doesn't erase older state filings, and data-broker sites may already have copied your home address. Removing it from those sites is a separate opt-out process.
- **Google Business Profile.** Google doesn't allow virtual or mailbox addresses as a storefront. If you set up a profile, register as a service-area business and hide the address.
- **Rules vary by state.** A few states have their own requirements for the principal office address. Check your Secretary of State's amendment form, which usually costs a small fee.
- This is general information, not legal advice.
