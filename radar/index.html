Why timing wins

The POS decision is made months before the sign goes up.

New owners pick a POS and a processor while they are still building out. One vendor timeline puts selection at 6 or more months before opening and installation at 2 to 3 months. Walk in after the grand opening and you are selling a switch.

The usual way

Bought lead lists

Mostly businesses that are already open, already under contract, and often already pitched by other agents. They go stale fast.

Also late

Spotting the grand opening

Scoring, explained

100 points, five plain rules.

No black box. Every lead shows how it earned each point, so you can skip the ones that do not fit your book.

Industry fit

35

Restaurants, food and bars score highest, then retail, beauty, fitness and auto. Read from NAICS codes where the filing has one, otherwise from the license type or business name.

Recency

25

Full points for filings in the last 7 days, tapering after that.

Physical storefront

15

Suites, plazas and centers score highest. PO boxes and missing addresses score zero. State filings lose a few points, since the address may be a home or an agent.

Independent owner

15

Full points when the name matches no known chain or franchise pattern. Chains and numbered franchise locations are dropped, because corporate usually picks the POS.

Pre-opening signal

10

Applications, pre-license inspections, build-out permits, and sales tax permits with a first sales date still ahead.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Something else on your mind? Ask in your reply after you join.

Where does the data come from?

Public open-data portals run by US cities and states: liquor license applications, sales tax permits, pre-license food inspections, new business licenses and registrations, and new state entity filings. There are 18 sources today. Every lead names the source it came from.

How fresh is it?

The digest runs every morning at 6:47 am Eastern and looks at filings from the last 7 days. Most portals update daily. Texas sales tax permits load weekly, Los Angeles updates monthly, and state registries lag 1 to 3 days. Two New York City datasets are stale at the source right now. Each digest includes source health, with the newest filing date for every portal.

Does every lead include an email or phone number?

No, and most do not. Public filings rarely list contact details. In one recent week, 188 of 1,651 qualified leads (about 11%) had an owner email on file, mostly from Connecticut's business registry, and 51 had a phone. Every lead includes an address plus links to Google Maps and the state business search so you can look up the owner. The rest are built for walk-ins and postcards.

Is this legal to use?

The filings are public records, and most open-data portals allow commercial reuse. How you reach out is what matters. Follow CAN-SPAM for email (the templates include your mailing address and an opt-out), dial business lines by hand, and do not cold text or use AI voice calls. A few states regulate commercial use of public records, so check the rules where you work. This is not legal advice.

Which cities and states are covered?

City sources: San Francisco, Chicago, Los Angeles, New York City, Seattle, New Orleans, Orlando and Norfolk. State sources: Texas, Colorado, Connecticut, New York State and Oregon. Coverage is strongest in Texas, New York, Connecticut, Colorado, Illinois and California. If your metro is thin or missing, request it when you join.

How is this different from buying a lead list?

Bought lists are mostly businesses that are already open and already have a processor, and they go stale fast. Storefront Radar finds businesses at the filing stage, often before they open, scores them for POS fit, and drops chains and numbered franchise locations, where corporate usually picks the POS. You get new names every morning instead of a one-time file.

Can I use it for other industries?

The filings cover every kind of new business, but the scoring is tuned for POS fit: restaurants, bars, retail, beauty, fitness and auto. If you sell insurance, payroll, bookkeeping or signage to new businesses, say so when you join. Other scoring profiles are on the roadmap and not available yet.
